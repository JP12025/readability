Charles Darwin was a young naturalist when he sailed around the world
on a ship called the Beagle. The trip took five years. During the
voyage, he collected plants, animals and fossils, and he wrote notes
about all that he saw.

On the Galapagos Islands, he noticed that the finches living on
different islands had beaks of different shapes. Birds that cracked
hard seeds had strong, thick beaks, while birds that caught insects had
thin, pointed ones. Why? Years later, Darwin explained these differences
with his theory of natural selection: individuals that are better adapted
to their environment survive longer and have more offspring.
